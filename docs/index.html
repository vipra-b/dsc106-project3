A Visual Investigation

Lightning Doesn't Light Most U.S. Wildfires

Folk belief blames lightning for wildfires. NOAA and the USDA say roughly 85 percent are human-caused. Here is what GOES-16 satellite data from the 2024 fire season shows about where lightning struck and where the country burned.

By Vipra Bindal  ·  DSC 106 Project 3

Fire pixels are real GOES-16 ABI-L2-FDCC detections (one snapshot per day at 20:00 UTC, binned to 0.5° cells). Lightning is real GOES-16 GLM-L2-LCFA flash detections, sampled 2 files per hour and scaled by 90 to estimate full-cadence counts.

Part 1 · Where

Where America burned, week by week

Each circle is a 0.5° cell where GOES-16 detected fire activity in a single week. The slider walks through every week from June through October — drag it to see how the fire footprint moves across the country.

Part 2 · The whole season

The 2024 fire season, accumulated

Now every week piles onto one map. The shape of the year emerges — the Sierra Nevada, the Mountain West, the Mexican border, a long Mississippi-Valley corridor of agricultural burning. The Northeast and Upper Midwest barely register.

Part 3 · By state

Aggregated to the state level

The dots dissolve. Each state lights up by its total fire radiative power for the season. The geography is impossible to miss: a Western band of intense red, a Texas-to-Florida corridor of moderate orange, and the rest of the country quiet.

Part 4 · The lightning overlay

Now overlay the lightning

The same map, recolored on two axes — fire on the vertical, lightning on the horizontal. The myth-buster reveals itself: the bright-red Western states are not the high-lightning states. Florida and the Gulf get the most lightning but barely burn. Click any state to see its weekly breakdown.

Part 5 · The verdict

One dot per fire, plotted against lightning

The X-axis is "lightning flashes within 24 hours before this fire was detected." If lightning caused fires, the dots would climb a diagonal. They don't. Drag any rectangle to focus a subset — the headline number tells you the share that had no preceding lightning. Whatever slice you take, the majority did.

Data: GOES-16 ABI-L2-FDCC (fires) and GOES-16 GLM-L2-LCFA (lightning), aggregated to 0.5° cells and weekly bins. U.S. state outlines from us-atlas.
