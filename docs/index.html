Lightning Doesn't Light Most U.S. Wildfires

Folk belief blames lightning for wildfires. NOAA and the USDA say roughly 85% are human-caused. Here is what GOES-16 satellite data (June–October 2024) shows about where lightning struck and where the country burned.

Draft notice. Fire pixels are real GOES-16 ABI-L2-FDCC detections (1 snapshot/day, 20:00 UTC, binned to 0.5° cells). Lightning is mock data with realistic CONUS climatology while the real GLM extract finishes.

Where America burned in 2024, week by week

Each circle is a 0.5° cell where GOES-16 detected fire activity in a given week. The slider walks through every week from June through October — drag it to see how the fire footprint moves across the country.

The full 2024 fire season, accumulated

Now all those weeks pile up onto one map. The shape of the season emerges — the Sierra Nevada, the Mountain West, the Mexican border, a long Mississippi-Valley corridor of agricultural burning. The Northeast and Upper Midwest barely register.

Aggregate to the state level

The dots dissolve. Each state lights up by its total fire radiative power for the season. Now the geography is impossible to miss: a Western band of intense red, a Texas-to-Florida corridor of moderate orange, and the rest of the country quiet.

Now overlay the lightning

Toggle "Overlay lightning data" below the map. The coloring switches to bivariate — fire activity on the vertical axis, lightning on the horizontal. The myth-buster reveals itself: the bright-red Western states are not the high-lightning states. Florida and the Gulf get the most lightning but barely burn. Click any state to see its weekly breakdown.

The verdict in one chart

One dot per fire-cell-week. The X-axis is "lightning flashes within 24 hours before this fire was detected." If lightning caused fires, the dots would climb a diagonal. They don't. The headline number updates as you brush or filter by region — the majority of fire cells have no preceding lightning, no matter how you slice it.

Data: GOES-16 ABI-L2-FDCC (fires) and GOES-16 GLM-L2-LCFA (lightning), aggregated to 0.5° cells and weekly bins. U.S. state outlines from us-atlas.
